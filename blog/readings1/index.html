Reading Set 1

Reading |

 18 February 2026

This reading added to this new perspective I’ve adopted when talking about the web that started last semester. As I become more cognizant of the “marketing” of the internet the more grounded I feel in my regard to it and its effects on our livelihood. I, like many others, didn’t know the infrastructure involved in the internet as a resource. Prior to a few semesters ago, I always thought that the internet was this intangible thing that travelled through cell towers wires from above and existed in this ethereal “cloud”. Since then, especially after this first set of reading, I’m starting to see a clear pattern forming: the way in which tech companies talk about and position the internet is intentional to leave the general public clueless on what is needed and at times sacrificed for “seamless” connections.

In many of my media classes we’ve talked about the leverage of information on a product especially when it's for monetary means. I think the fact that internet connectivity is leveraged as a commodity rather than a human right, is shown in the hidden infrastructure. Most of the heavy lifting involved with connecting different parts of the world is done by these undersea fiber-optic cables. And yet, these cables are strategically left out of most conversations around wireless connection. I think a lot of that has to do with leaving the general public in the dark. For me, I felt I became a little more grounded in my understanding of the internet after learning about the undersea cables. I think this redirection of our attention upwards rather than downwards is used by tech companies to lobby for “innovation.” This meta look doesn’t acknowledge the worldly limitations (natural resources, the livelihood of people) and instead tries to rationalize these things as collateral all in the pursuit of this technological transcendence. I don’t know how much of this omission could be classified as misinformation, but it’s clear that tech companies monopolize off of the general public’s ignorance of the internet as a structure.

This also frames the internet as this neutral entity which is not true at all. The infrastructure of the internet seems to be an issue that most are not aware affects them until it already has. Calling the internet “wireless” makes it seem like a resource anyone has access to. It also makes it seem open source to any and all that know how to access it: not owned or controlled. It frames it as though not being able to access the internet is an issue on the user rather than a deliberate infrastructural choice. It also overwrites the colonial and military history as to where the cables were to be laid. A “wireless” view allows for accountability to go unremediated.
