 15 April 2026

Alot of these apps adorn a semblance of user safety when in actuality, the landscpae is actually more harmful than user's are aware. If it weren't for moderators constantly deleting content on a daily basis the social media apps we use would look so different. However, I think that this deleted media truly shows the current internet culture at the moment. The deeper part of the iceberg that goes unnoticed but is the biggest piece.

I think that the shirt from the internet being community-oriented to being capital-oriented played a huge part in this. "But it’s also a design choice, engineered to make us care about social platforms by concealing from us those who care after them." This invisibility puts too much credit on the social apps and the stakeholders they use (Facebook --> Mark Zuck; Twitter: Elon) when they are not the people who actually have to look at the content that needs to be deleted.

Content aside I think that big Social also feels really formulaic like people are doing the exact thing as others. I have thought about the idea of scaling down social media platforms in the past. I think there's a reason why forum platform like Reddit last so long. Though reddit does extend teh capacity for "intimate" community. With forums, though, there's this small groups within one big group structure that allows for smaller communities to engage deeply with one another. But then I think of exclusive apps like the Tea App. It was created for women only and was referral/ invite only accessible. The app was created to help women know about the backgrounds of the men they were dating for safety purposes. But I have seen a rise in exclusive apps that I think help this discussion of smaller internet spaces/ apps.
