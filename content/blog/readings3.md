The readings talk about how the web and the technology that we use have changed from something we’d act upon to something acting upon us and shaping us as a result. The earliest version of the internet had a more rigid build to it that allowed for easy manipulation from the user. From how I understand it, the internet was a clear set of binary, what could and couldn't work. However, the internet we have today is more approximation based. There is this seal of “certainty” that is really just fabrications of knowledge. If something “sounds” correct then it must be the correct sort of thinking. These readings further cemented my stance that the internet is then not neutral or ambiguous. The bias within it stems from the users and builders and in turn is within the information architecture. The leveraging of convenience really sells this point. 

I think the part of the readings that stuck with me is the negotiated stance the authors take. I think in a lot of conversations about the internet some who critique take this “holier than tho” approach as if they don't use the technology. However, in the readings the audience talk about the real advantages of the internet. Convenience on the web is not the issue but rather the excessiveness of it. Not everything needs a short cut. If anything this convenience model just makes it easier for companies to exploit the dependence the user then grows. This conversation though is important because it creates a more engaged relationship with technology. I think this engagement sort of adds some balance to an otherwise skewed relationship.
